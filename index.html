About this sheet

Normal probability paper rescales the cumulative-probability axis by the standard normal quantile function \Phi^{-1}, so its rulings crowd together towards the tails and open out around 50%. If X \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2), its quantiles satisfy x_p = \mu + \sigma\,\Phi^{-1}(p): on this scale a normal population is a straight line with intercept \mu and slope \sigma.

Ordered observations that bend away from a straight line therefore signal a departure from normality: a convex curve points to right skew, a flattened S to tails lighter than the normal. The rulings on this page are computed rather than drawn by eye, using Acklam's rational approximation to \Phi^{-1}.
