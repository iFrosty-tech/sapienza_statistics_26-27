§1 Problem statement

The assignment reads, verbatim:

Consider n systems and m attackers. Model the penetration process using a stochastic process with independent and identically distributed increments X₁, X₂, …, representing the penetration score at each step.

Bernoulli model: Xᵢ ∈ {0,1}, with P(Xᵢ = 1) = p.

Rademacher model: Xᵢ ∈ {−1,+1}, with P(Xᵢ = +1) = P(Xᵢ = −1) = 1/2. This second model is introduced to study the symmetric, zero-mean case.

For increasing values of n and suitable choices of m, simulate multiple sample trajectories and study the distributions of the following three quantities:

  • Total score: Sₙ = ∑ᵢ₌₁ⁿ Xᵢ
  • Average score: Sₙ/n = (1/n)∑ᵢ₌₁ⁿ Xᵢ
  • Normalized total score: Sₙ/√n = (1/√n)∑ᵢ₌₁ⁿ Xᵢ

Compare the behavior of these quantities as n increases. In particular, investigate the convergence in probability of Sₙ/n according to the Law of Large Numbers, the behavior of the variance under the different normalizations, and the convergence in distribution of Sₙ/√n to a standard normal distribution in the Rademacher case, according to the Central Limit Theorem.

Interpret the simulated trajectories in the context of the n systems and m attackers, discussing how the system size and number of attackers affect the aggregate penetration score.

Each attacker is modelled as one sample trajectory: attacker j attacks the n systems in sequence and X_i^{(j)} is its score on system i, so that S_n^{(j)} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i^{(j)} is its penetration score after n systems. The number of systems n is therefore the length of a trajectory and the number of attackers m the number of independent trajectories, the Monte Carlo sample from which the law of S_n is estimated. The study runs {{fig.params.m}} attackers over up to {{fig.params.nMax}} systems in each model.

§2 The model

2.1 Increments

Both increments take one of two values and can be written as an affine image of a Bernoulli variable:

X_i = a + (b - a)\,B_i, \qquad B_i \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(q), \qquad \mu = \mathbb{E}[X_i] = a + (b - a)\,q, \qquad \sigma^2 = \mathrm{Var}(X_i) = (b - a)^2\,q\,(1 - q),
(1)

with a = 0, b = 1, q = p in the Bernoulli model, so that \mu = p and \sigma^2 = p(1 - p), and a = -1, b = 1, q = 1/2 in the Rademacher model, so that \mu = 0 and \sigma^2 = 1. The study takes p = {{fig.params.p}}: \mu = {{fig.theory.bernoulli.mean}} and \sigma^2 = {{fig.theory.bernoulli.variance}}.

2.2 The exact law of the total score

Summing (1) over the n systems gives the total score as an affine image of a binomial count, whose law is therefore known exactly:

S_n = n a + (b - a)\,K_n, \quad K_n \sim \mathrm{Bin}(n, q), \qquad \Pr\{S_n = n a + (b - a)k\} = \binom{n}{k} q^k (1 - q)^{n - k}, \quad k = 0, \dots, n .
(2)

Every simulated distribution on this page is set against (2), so the study separates what the simulation shows from what it can only estimate.

2.3 Moments of the three quantities

By linearity and independence,

\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}[S_n] &= n\mu, & \mathrm{Var}(S_n) &= n\sigma^2, \\ \mathbb{E}[S_n/n] &= \mu, & \mathrm{Var}(S_n/n) &= \sigma^2/n, \\ \mathbb{E}[S_n/\sqrt{n}] &= \sqrt{n}\,\mu, & \mathrm{Var}(S_n/\sqrt{n}) &= \sigma^2 . \end{aligned}
(3)

§5 Variance under the three normalisations

Dividing S_n by n^{\alpha} multiplies its variance by n^{-2\alpha}:

\mathrm{Var}\!\left(\frac{S_n}{n^{\alpha}}\right) = \sigma^2\, n^{1 - 2\alpha}, \qquad \log_{10} \mathrm{Var}\!\left(\frac{S_n}{n^{\alpha}}\right) = \log_{10}\sigma^2 + (1 - 2\alpha)\log_{10} n ,
(4)

a straight line of slope +1, -1 and 0 on log–log axes for \alpha = 0, 1 and 1/2. Figure 4 plots the sample variances of the {{fig.params.m}} attackers at the {{fig.params.curveCount}} values n = {{fig.params.curveN}}; Table 2 gives the least-squares slopes and constants.

§6 Law of large numbers

The weak law of large numbers states that S_n/n converges in probability to \mu: for every \varepsilon > 0, \Pr\{|S_n/n - \mu| \ge \varepsilon\} \to 0 as n \to \infty. Two inequalities bound this probability at finite n:

\Pr\left\{\left|\frac{S_n}{n} - \mu\right| \ge \varepsilon\right\} \le \frac{\sigma^2}{n\varepsilon^2} \quad\text{(Chebyshev)}, \qquad \Pr\left\{\left|\frac{S_n}{n} - \mu\right| \ge \varepsilon\right\} \le 2\exp\!\left(-\frac{2n\varepsilon^2}{(b - a)^2}\right) \quad\text{(Hoeffding)} .
(5)

Figure 5 compares them with the exact probability, computed from (2), and with the proportion of the {{fig.params.m}} attackers whose average deviates by at least \varepsilon, for \varepsilon = {{fig.params.eps1}} and {{fig.params.eps2}}.

§7 Central limit theorem

7.1 The Rademacher case

In the Rademacher model \mu = 0 and \sigma = 1, so the normalised score is the standardised sum and the central limit theorem states that S_n/\sqrt{n} \to N(0, 1) in distribution. Its rate is measured by the Kolmogorov distance, bounded by the Berry–Esseen inequality:

D_n = \sup_{x}\left|\Pr\left\{\frac{S_n - n\mu}{\sigma\sqrt{n}} \le x\right\} - \Phi(x)\right| \le \frac{C\,\rho}{\sigma^3\sqrt{n}}, \qquad \rho = \mathbb{E}|X_1 - \mu|^3, \quad C \le 0.4748 .
(6)

For a sample of m attackers the empirical distribution function lies within \sqrt{\ln(2/\alpha)/(2m)} of the true one with probability at least 1 - \alpha (the Dvoretzky–Kiefer–Wolfowitz inequality with Massart's constant): {{fig.clt.dkw}} for m = {{fig.params.m}} at the {{fig.params.dkwLevel}} level. Below this floor the simulation cannot resolve D_n, and the exact law (2) takes over.

7.2 The Bernoulli case

In the Bernoulli model S_n/\sqrt{n} has mean p\sqrt{n}, which grows without bound, so it has no limit law; it is the standardised score (S_n - np)/\sqrt{np(1 - p)} that converges to N(0, 1), more slowly the more skewed the increment. Figure 8 shows both.

§8 Systems and attackers

8.1 The number of attackers as the size of the sample

Under the reading of §1 the number of attackers m does not change the law of S_n; it sets how precisely the simulation estimates it. To show this at n = {{fig.params.precisionN}}, the attackers are split into disjoint groups of m = {{fig.params.groupSizes}}, and each group estimates the mean average score and the distribution function of S_n.

8.2 The aggregate penetration score

The aggregate score of m attackers over n systems, T = \sum_{j=1}^{m} S_n^{(j)}, is a sum of mn independent increments, so its law depends on m and n only through their product:

\mathbb{E}[T] = mn\mu, \qquad \mathrm{Var}(T) = mn\sigma^2, \qquad T = mna + (b - a)\,K, \quad K \sim \mathrm{Bin}(mn, q) .
(7)

8.3 The best attacker

The aggregate hides the extremes. In the Bernoulli model S_n counts the systems an attacker penetrates, and the chance that at least one of m independent attackers penetrates at least a share p + \delta of them is

\Pr\left\{\max_{1 \le j \le m} S_n^{(j)} \ge \lceil (p + \delta)\,n \rceil\right\} = 1 - \Pr\{S_n < \lceil (p + \delta)\,n \rceil\}^{m} ,
(8)

with \delta = {{fig.best.excess}}, a share of {{fig.best.share}}. Figure 10 plots (8) against m and Table 7 gives the number of attackers for an even chance.

§9 Discussion

The three quantities answer three different questions about the same attackers. The total score measures how much an attacker has achieved, the average score its rate of success per system, and the normalised score the size of its luck relative to the typical fluctuation.

App. Reproducibility

Every figure, table and statistic on this page is computed when the page is built, from the parameters and seeds of Table A1: npm test runs the test suite, which covers the exact laws, the bounds, the simulation and the study, and npm run build rebuilds the page. The study draws {{fig.params.draws}} pseudo-random numbers and takes about {{fig.params.computeSeconds}} seconds (last computed {{fig.params.computedAt}}); its results are cached under node_modules/.cache/hw04/ with the key {{fig.params.cacheKey}}, a hash of the parameters, of the source of every module involved and of the figure provider itself, so that a change to any of them recomputes everything.

Each model has its own stream, derived from the master seed {{fig.params.seed}} by a 32-bit hash of a label: {{fig.params.seeds.bernoulli}} for the Bernoulli run and {{fig.params.seeds.rademacher}} for the Rademacher run. Within a run the attackers draw from one Mulberry32 stream one after another, attacker j taking draws jN + 1, \dots, (j + 1)N for the largest n = N, so the scores at every smaller n are those of the same attackers and no two attackers share a draw.

  • src/lib/penetration-study.js: the experiments of §3–§8.
  • src/lib/penetration-charts.js: the figures and tables.
  • homework/04/figures.js: the parameters, seeds and sizes of every figure on this page.
